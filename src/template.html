Layer four, the perimeter

An instrument that thinks in layers.

Kythera is named after the Antikythera mechanism, the oldest known analogue computer. Like that bronze stack of gears, it is five nested layers, each doing one kind of work. It never settles: what you see is its state right now, not a loop.

Layer three, the lattice

Four layers, four verbs.

The layers are separated here so you can see them. Hover a verb to light the layer that does it.

  • Perceive

    Reads its surroundings

    Samples signal at its edge, including your cursor, and bends toward it.

  • Infer

    Locks onto patterns

    When loose motion holds a shape long enough, it snaps into a lattice.

  • Route

    Holds worlds in orbit

    Sends energy between distant nodes, and keeps seven worlds turning around it.

  • Retain

    Keeps state between rebuilds

    The core never restarts from zero; each configuration inherits the last.
